## Organisational identity and purpose

### What “Skål” means in this knowledge file

Skål International is an international, non-profit professional association for travel and tourism professionals. Its distinguishing proposition is to connect people across the different branches of travel and tourism through professional relationships, friendship, networking, responsible tourism and mutual support. It describes its positioning as **“Doing Business Among Friends.”** Its members are known as **Skålleagues**. Membership is personal; the association is not simply a directory of tourism companies. [S22, PDF pp. 4–6; S29, PDF pp. 2, 7–10]

The uploaded material documents **Skål International the association**, its existing website/member portal, its governance and its programmes. It does not establish a technology stack, database schema, API, deployment architecture or implementation status for the GitHub repository `moritzfreise/Skal`. References to a portal, app, AI search or digital platform describe association services or source claims; they are not evidence that a separate software project has implemented them. [S01–S11, S13–S16, S20, website tutorials; S29, PDF pp. 7–10; S40, §§5, 11]

### Vision, mission and operating principles

- **Vision:** “To be a trusted voice in travel and tourism.”
- **Mission:** Through leadership, professionalism and friendship, work together to achieve the vision, maximise networking opportunities and support a responsible tourism industry.
- **Professional purpose:** Recruit qualified travel and tourism managers, develop professional relationships, and support the professional development of people working in or training for the industry, especially young people.
- **Friendship and solidarity:** Encourage mutual understanding, peace, integrity and friendship across national boundaries; support distressed members and eligible families through the Florimond Volckaert Fund.
- **Participation:** Encourage voluntary contribution beyond minimum membership obligations; members may pursue professional and social goals through Skål.
- **Non-discrimination:** The Statutes prohibit social, political, labour-union, religious, race, sex or age discrimination. Political, union or religious questions that endanger friendship or unity should be carefully avoided in club life.
- **Non-profit:** Association income and assets serve the mission; member contributions are not shares and confer no personal ownership rights over the association's wealth. Direct commercial relationships between members and supporters are encouraged.
- **Democratic and autonomous:** Clubs and National/Area Committees operate autonomously within international rules. The General Secretariat manages resources, programmes and activities under Executive Board oversight.
- **Community-facing and international:** Cooperate with education, culture, tourism-training and peace-oriented institutions and local communities; expand the benefits of the movement to eligible tourism professionals worldwide.

Sources: [S22, PDF pp. 4–6]. The position statement describes an opportunity for business and friendship, not a guarantee of business leads, contracts, financial returns or unconditional assistance.

### Origin and historical interpretation

**Primary confirmation:** The Statutes' subtitle and the March 2025 brochure place the foundation of Skål International in **1934**. The brochure describes the association as connecting travel and tourism professionals for over 90 years. [S22, PDF p. 4; S29, PDF pp. 2, 5]

**Detailed history reported by the supplied secondary synthesis:** Parisian travel agents formed friendships during early-1930s aviation familiarisation travel along an Amsterdam–Copenhagen–Malmö route. Paris club formation is dated to December 1932; named founders include Jules Mohr, Florimond Volckaert, Hugo Krafft, Pierre Soulié and Georges Ithier. The international association is dated to **28 April 1934**, at the Hôtel Scribe in Paris, with Florimond Volckaert as its first President. The synthesis identifies 28 April as **World Skål Day** and “Skål” as a Scandinavian toast. Exact attendance figures, founding-session dates and the route's carrier details should be checked against a primary history publication before authoritative historical use. [S40, §§1–2]

Do not confuse **the first club's 1932 origin** with **the international association's 1934 foundation**. Older strategic material uses “Executive Committee” and “International Skål Council”; its terminology reflects the organisational framework or proposals of its own period. For example, the Skål 2020 presentation is explicitly dated **28 October 2014** and describes survey findings and project proposals, not a 2020 or 2026 implementation report. [S41, slides 1–14]

3. **International travel contacts:** Meet fellow members and connect with clubs when travelling.
4. **Trade-show presence:** Use a Skål booth or meeting point and connect with members/business contacts at events such as FITUR, WTM Africa, WTM London, IMEX Frankfurt, IMEX America and IBTM World. The examples are brochure-era opportunities, not promises of a booth at every later edition.
5. **Annual World Congress:** International networking, B2B sessions, workshops, seminars and leisure activities.

8. **Member offers:** Discounts, special rates and upgrades through the member-benefits section, subject to the individual offer's conditions.
9. **Solidarity:** Access to the Florimond Volckaert Fund's assistance process in qualifying circumstances; assistance is subject to fund rules.
10. **Information and platforms:** Newsletters, magazines, website/social media, and digital engagement described in the brochure, including reference to a Skål App.
11. **Recognition and collaboration:** Sustainable Tourism Awards and sponsorship/partnership opportunities.

Sources: [S29, PDF pp. 6–11]. Membership fees, event registration, travel costs, platform availability and offer conditions require their own current confirmation. A brochure's statement that an app exists does not establish its present download location, features or availability.

## Membership, eligibility and rights

### Membership is personal; category, industry classification and distinction differ

The Statutes list six personal membership categories: **Active, Active Individual, Allied, Retired, Life and Young Skål**. Young Skål has **Skål Student** and **Young Skål Professional** subcategories. An industry classification explains the member's professional qualification; an honorary title recognises service; business membership is a locally managed arrangement; corporate affiliation is a sponsorship/benefits arrangement. These are not interchangeable membership categories. New admissions are limited to qualified Active, Active Individual, Allied and Young Skål candidates. Retired is normally a change from an existing qualifying membership; Life is closed to new admissions. [S22, PDF p. 6; S24, PDF pp. 4–6, 11–12]

### Categories and practical eligibility

### Eligible industry sectors: use the April 2026 classification

Active candidates must satisfy both the managerial-position requirement and the detailed sector definitions. Named positions include President/Chair/Owner/CEO, Vice President/Managing Director/Director General/General Manager, their assistant/deputy equivalents, Director/Deputy Director and Manager/Deputy Manager; relevant departments include sales, marketing, publicity, public/guest relations and revenue. Being employed by a company that merely has some tourism customers does not by itself establish eligibility. [S24, PDF p. 6]

1. **Accommodation:** hotel groups/brands and accommodation providers. Tourist apartments and holiday guest houses are expressly excluded in this classification. [S24, PDF p. 6]
2. **Attractions and experiences:** tourism-oriented golf/resorts; structured gastronomy/beverage tourism; permanent leisure, heritage and entertainment attractions; medical tourism; nature/adventure tourism; restaurants with a demonstrated significant tourism clientele; sport tourism; visitor-oriented wellness, spa and retreats. Exclusions include resident-focused golf clubs, bars/simple product tastings without structured visitor experience, catering companies, individual guides/instructors, local non-tourism amusements, purely local neighbourhood restaurants, beverage-led nightlife/fast food, and chefs/maîtres unless proprietors. Medical-tourism facilitators/case executives have specific Medical Tourism Association registration requirements. [S24, PDF p. 7]
3. **Operational and strategic services:** dedicated congress/exhibition venues, professional congress/events organisers, tourism-exclusive consulting, tourism-exclusive executives inside otherwise non-tourism organisations, travel-only insurance, primarily tourism marketing/communications agencies, and tourism-exclusive technology/booking platforms. Exclusions include multi-sector generalist providers, local/community event activity, general insurance/credit-card companies merely offering travel insurance, and generalist technology adapted for tourism. An applicant excluded from Active classification is not automatically eligible for Allied: assess Allied's separate requirements. [S24, PDF p. 8]
4. **Travel services:** event-based arts/culture with clear tourism impact; DMCs; inbound/outbound tour operators; travel agencies/OTAs. Travel brokers must be contracted with an IATA-registered organisation such as a wholesaler, consolidator or tour operator. Individual artists, purely local events and fixed attractions belonging in the attractions classification are not included as arts/event organisers. [S24, PDF p. 9]
5. **Transportation:** passenger airlines/airports and passenger-airline GSAs; licensed tourism vehicle rental/mobility; tourism coach/bus operators; cruise/water passenger transport, tourism yachts and relevant ports; tourism/long-distance passenger rail. Exclusions include cargo/freight-only operators, taxis/ride-hailing, individual couriers/guides providing transport, and predominantly local commuter bus/rail systems. [S24, PDF pp. 9–10]
6. **Tourist and travel organisations:** accommodation/hospitality associations; DMOs, CVBs, tourism authorities, NTOs, tourist boards and destination-governance bodies; travel trade/MICE associations; tourism-linked passenger-transport associations. Primarily local non-tourism public transport is excluded. [S24, PDF p. 10]
7. **Media and education:** tourism certification bodies; accredited tourism/hospitality education; tourism leadership speakers/executive coaches with documented senior-sector experience; established travel media/digital publishers. Academic representatives include Dean, Principal, Director, department/faculty Head and permanent senior lecturers; not visiting/part-time lecturers or unrelated general staff. Eligible media representatives are full-time publishers, editors-in-chief, journalists, producers or broadcasters dedicated to tourism/travel; personal/non-editorial influencers, bloggers and freelance/part-time writers are excluded. General ISO bodies and generic motivational speakers without tourism credentials are excluded. [S24, PDF pp. 10–11]

### Admission, changes, transfers and reinstatement

**Admission workflow:** submit a valid Membership Proposal Form for every proposed Active, Active Individual, Allied or Young Skål member; local Club approval is followed by Skål International approval and notification before membership becomes effective. Even an eligible candidate remains subject to the Club's right of admission. President and Secretary verify the truth and accuracy of application information; approval of false information can have ethical consequences. A National Committee may add validation for financial/operational oversight after formally notifying the Secretariat. If rejected, the Club informs the Secretariat of the reason. New members sign a data-protection privacy policy. [S24, PDF p. 11; S22, PDF p. 6]

**Transfer:** a member changing residence/business applies through the current Club for transfer to the destination Club; the By-laws say the destination Club must accept the member in the existing category. If annual fee was not paid at origin, it is paid to the destination. If no Club is reasonably close, the rules provide for Active Individual status; check category suitability rather than automatically reclassifying a Retired/Student member without Secretariat advice. [S24, PDF p. 12]

**Lapse/reinstatement:** reinstatement in the same year or the year after removal can waive administration fee and preserve seniority if category requirements remain satisfied. Outside the reinstatement window, a new proposal form is required. The text in Article I 1.5(b) awkwardly overlaps its form rule and reinstatement exception; Article X 1(b)(vi) expressly confirms the same-year/following-year reinstatement window. Non-payment causes suspension; payment of the amount due within the current subscription year restores membership. [S24, PDF pp. 12, 27]

**Business membership:** a tourism company may nominate a qualifying Active member in its local Club and replace that person if they leave employment, paying the international administration fee for the replacement. The departing individual may retain Active membership by paying that year's membership fee; administration fee is waived to keep that person active. This locally administered arrangement does not make personal membership a transferable corporate property right. [S24, PDF p. 12]

### Corporate affiliation is separate from personal membership

The supplied April 2026 schedule lists annual Bronze **€5,000**, Silver **€10,000**, Gold **€15,000**, and Platinum **from €30,000**. Platinum is negotiated only by the President and/or Vice President. All levels have company-linked visibility and a specific Corporate Affiliate logo; higher tiers add newsletter/magazine advertising, Congress registrations and, for Gold, a Congress B2B booth. International annual fees are waived for one qualifying company member at Bronze, two at Silver and the first five at Gold; personal eligibility still applies and Club/National fees remain payable. National sponsorship benefits stay local/national unless an international affiliation is purchased. Treat these as the supplied 2026 schedule, not a guaranteed future quotation. [S24, PDF p. 6]

## Governance and organisational authority

### Legal identity and governing hierarchy

The legal association is **Association Internationale des Skål Clubs (A.I.S.C.)**, known as **Skål International**, subtitled **International Association of Travel and Tourism Professionals – Founded in 1934**. It is an international, non-profit association legally registered in Spain; the Statutes cite Spanish Organic Law 1/2002 and applicable accounting rules. Its registered address in the supplied Statutes is **Edificio España, Avenida Palma de Mallorca 15, 1º Planta, 29620 Torremolinos, Spain**. The association owns the Skål International trademark worldwide and licenses its use under its regulations. [S22, PDF p. 4]

The authority order is **Statutes → International By-laws → other official regulations/manuals and local regulations**. The Statutes explicitly prevail if documents conflict. The supplied Statutes are effective **28 September 2025**; the supplied By-laws are effective **April 2026**. A later manual does not automatically override a higher-authority rule. In conflict with national law, refer the documented issue to Skål International for resolution; do not assume a Club can silently disregard either its law or international rules. Official languages are **English, French and Spanish**. Language-version interpretation disputes are resolved by the Executive Board; a disputed interpretation can be referred to the Assembly. Local-language translations are unofficial and lack legal standing. [S22, PDF pp. 3–4, 11; S24, PDF pp. 3–4, 32]

### The organisational units

- **Club:** basic geographic unit and member's normal route for opinions/proposals on Skål matters. Clubs welcome visiting Skålleagues, promote Skål and comply with administration/finance obligations. [S22, PDF pp. 6–7; S24, PDF p. 12]
- **National Committee (NC):** must normally be formed in countries with four or more Clubs; optional with two or three; geographical/other exceptions require Executive Board discretion. Clubs belong to their geographically/politically appropriate NC. [S24, PDF p. 14]
- **Area Committee:** approved association of NCs and/or Affiliated Clubs for international cooperation; membership is not compulsory. [S24, PDF p. 14]
- **Affiliated Club:** every Club is internationally affiliated; the special usage describes a country's sole Club outside an NC. It has equal Club standing and may join an Area Committee. [S24, PDF pp. 13–14]
- **General Secretariat:** paid administrative staff, accountable to and overseen by the Executive Board; administers membership, records, finances and programmes. It is not the elected governing Board. [S22, PDF pp. 5–6]
- **Executive Board (EB):** elected governing body accountable to the General Assembly; President represents the association. [S22, PDF pp. 6–8]
- **General Assembly of Club Delegates:** supreme authority; members participate through Clubs, and only accredited voting delegates cast international Club votes. [S22, PDF pp. 6, 9–10; S24, PDF pp. 23–24]

### Club formation, continuing obligations and termination

A new Club needs **at least 15 Active founding members**. It must maintain at least **10 Active, Allied, Life, Retired and Young Skål Professional members** to remain voting-eligible and avoid suspension/termination. New Club documentation and payment go through the Secretariat; the Executive Board admits the Club. Clubs in the same country may merge with NC and international approval; the surviving Club retains the lowest number, and other numbers are suspended and recoverable on approved reinstatement. [S24, PDF p. 13]

Each Club must hold an annual general assembly within applicable local/national legal timing and **at least five other official functions annually**; have a committee of at least **four**; comply with approved model provisions except documented legal conflicts; remit dues; and update local statutes within **90 days** of international amendments, sending them for Secretariat approval. Office terms start **1 January after election** unless local/national law provides otherwise. A Club President may combine other Club offices except Treasurer and other National/Area offices except President or Treasurer; NC/Area President cannot hold another office at Club/NC/Area level. Other office combinations are permitted, but no more than two concurrent offices is recommended. [S24, PDF pp. 13, 15]

Only the international Executive Board can suspend/terminate a Club. Grounds include dues arrears, membership below ten, ceasing to function/exist or regulatory breaches. On cessation, a Club must stop using Skål names, branding and insignia and close its Skål website/social accounts; Statutes also prohibit further posting to jointly managed/other Skål accounts. Reactivation requires restored obligations and international approval. Do not confuse voluntary resignation, suspension, termination and temporary suspension of a merged Club number. [S22, PDF p. 7; S24, PDF p. 13]

### National and Area administration

NC/Area bodies require Executive Board approval of formation, composition and statutes; their committee has at least **five members**, normally President, one/two Vice Presidents, Secretary and Treasurer. Elected officers must be Active, Life or Retired, subject to the Retired-office conflict below. Their AGM may be virtual when circumstances prevent meeting in person; hybrid arrangements must certify attendance and votes and have at least two physically present. Annual meeting/activity reports go to the Secretariat within **one month**, in an official language. Duties include Club development/support, new Club formation, compliance, liaison, regional coordination and internationalism. NCs retain direct correspondence with Skål International even when part of an Area Committee. [S24, PDF pp. 14–15]

### Executive Board composition and working rules

The Board has **14 members: President, Vice President/President-Elect and 12 Directors**; it represents **14 Regions**, including the Regions of the President and Vice President. Do not describe it as 14 ordinary Directors plus a separate President and Vice President. Board members cannot hold another Skål position at any level; no derogations. Regional Directors are ex-officio **non-voting** participants in their NC/Area bodies. Deputies are not extra voting Board seats. [S22, PDF pp. 7–8; S24, PDF pp. 15–16, 19–22]

The Board must hold at least **two in-person and eight virtual meetings annually**; quorum is **eight**. Each Board member has one vote; normal decisions require a simple majority of valid votes excluding abstentions, with **a tied Board motion defeated**. Urgent written/electronic votes close in **48 hours** if eight members responded, or earlier if all voted. Meetings are recorded; in-camera sessions have safeguarded written minutes. Minutes go to Board members within 30 days subject to the stated receipt condition, are confirmed at the next meeting and then published with confidential/personal information removed. Board debates/votes/proceedings remain confidential. [S24, PDF pp. 15–16]

The Board governs within regulations and Assembly directives; summons Assemblies, implements decisions, appoints staff/auditors, approves other official regulations, arbitrates disputes and oversees finances. Legally binding decisions require Board decision/minuting and President signature. One elected Director becomes Finance Director following Board vetting. Board members are unpaid volunteers with justified expense reimbursement. Directors provide regional liaison, strategic oversight, ethical leadership and portfolio/committee delivery. Deputy Directors meet their Director monthly, support regional cooperation, may co-chair a committee if appointed and replace the Director on vacancy under election rules. [S22, PDF pp. 8–9; S24, PDF pp. 16–17; S32, PDF pp. 1–2]

### Standing committees

The six standing committees in April 2026 are **Finance; Governance, Statutes and By-laws; Sustainability; Media, Public Relations, Branding and Marketing; Technology; and Membership Development, Membership Development Fund and Florimond Volckaert Fund**. They advise the Board through their Director/Chair; ad-hoc/time-bound committees may also be created. Membership is normally one year with reapplication, skill-based selection, ideally 8–14 people with broad Regional representation, no Region in a majority and at most two committees per member. Meetings should occur at least four times yearly according to workload; quorum is **50% + 1**. Minutes are documented, distributed and posted. A Past Presidents Advisory Panel may be convened for a defined issue/duration. [S24, PDF pp. 17–18]

### Assembly voting and the annual election timetable

International votes belong to **eligible Clubs through their confirmed delegate**, not to every individual member. Default delegate is Club President; a replacement must be on that Club's Executive Board. Confirmation is required **four weeks before virtual elections**; changes within two weeks are refused except force majeure. Membership counts for voting are calculated four weeks before virtual elections from paid Active, Allied, Life, Retired and Young Skål Professional members. At **10–65** qualifying paid members a Club has one vote; at **more than 65**, two; it never gains more than two under this rule. The one delegate casts a single ballot weighted as two when appropriate. Unpaid dues, fewer than ten qualifying members or no timely delegate confirmation means no vote. One Club cannot transfer its vote to another Club's delegate. [S24, PDF pp. 22–24]

Ordinary decisions exclude abstentions; with two choices the higher count wins, with more than two choices the rule is **50% + 1** and possible runoff. Non-Board ties are repeated until resolved. Statute amendments require two-thirds of votes cast excluding abstentions. **Dissolution is different:** at least two-thirds of **all active Clubs** must vote in favour, and **each Club has only one vote** regardless of size. Do not confuse these denominators. [S24, PDF pp. 4, 24; S22, PDF pp. 11–12]

### Finances and audit controls

Annual international dues are per-member Club obligations, set by Assembly and separate from Club/NC charges; do not invent a universal individual price. Life, Retired and Young Skål enjoy reduced rates. Dues are due **1 January**, latest payment **28 February**; unpaid dues after **1 March** incur a Board-set administration charge and/or interest. By-laws specify suspension for Clubs still unpaid by **31 May**, with automatic reactivation on payment within that calendar year. Another general suspension clause allows action after three months from due date, so these clauses should not be treated as a promise of uninterrupted rights until May. Invoicing uses the membership database at invoicing time. [S24, PDF pp. 13, 27]

The financial year is **1 January–31 December**; Finance Director closes accounts by **15 February**. Contracts above **€5,000** need prior minuted Board approval. Payments need **two joint signatures**. Normal Secretariat office expenses within approved budget/purpose do not require extra Finance Director approval; unbudgeted/over-line expenditure does. Travel expenses require Finance Director approval; that Director's travel is approved by President. Copies of contracts involving **€7,500 or more** must be available to a Club on written request; this disclosure threshold differs from contract-approval threshold. The Reserve Fund is controlled by the General Assembly, with a specified credit-card guarantee authorisation/reporting exception. [S24, PDF pp. 17, 27; S22, PDF pp. 8, 11]

Two Internal Auditors plus Deputy report independently to the Assembly, outside the Board, alongside a professional external auditor. Internal review is quarterly and the audit/report must be completed by **31 May**. Auditor reports cannot be amended by Board members. Serious irregularities go immediately in writing to President, copied to Vice President; financial-loss issues can justify an extraordinary Assembly. Internal/Deputy Auditors cannot hold another Skål office or be employed/contracted by any Skål body. [S22, PDF pp. 10–11; S24, PDF p. 28]

Auditor candidates must be Active/Life, fluent in English, members for the six immediately preceding years, experienced elected officers with financial background; former international Board members are ineligible. One candidate per Region; nomination deadline 75 days before AGA, regional selection if needed at least 55 days before, announcement 50 days before. Signed conduct/interest documents are required. Normal AGA election ranks candidates by votes: highest Junior Auditor, next Deputy, with automatic Junior-to-Senior advancement next year. Different ranking applies when both Auditor seats are vacant. Auditor term is two years, renewable once (four years total); Deputy one-year terms to four total. [S24, PDF pp. 28–29]

### Amendments and dissolution

Statutes are ordinarily reviewed on a five-year cycle beginning with 2023; Board may bring necessary earlier amendments. Club/NC/Area proposals go to Secretariat **four months** before AGA, Board proposals **two months**; extraordinary-Assembly proposals **30 days** prior. Circulation is at least **six weeks** before AGA or **two weeks** before extraordinary Assembly. Approved amendments take effect immediately and are not retroactive. [S22, PDF pp. 11–12]

Board-approved By-laws amendments take immediate effect after approved procedure; no amendment proposal is allowed in the two months immediately before an Assembly. By-laws allow opposition by at least **5% of total Clubs from at least five countries**, within **45 days** after notification, subject to the earlier pre-Assembly deadline; opposed clauses are held in abeyance for Assembly decision. Statutes specify the 5% threshold without that extra five-country condition, so eligibility restrictions need authority review. [S24, PDF p. 32; S22, PDF p. 11]

Dissolution must be an explicit AGA/extraordinary-Assembly purpose and requires two-thirds of all active Clubs voting in favour, one Club/one vote. A three-person liquidation committee pays debts and donates remaining funds to benevolent non-profit bodies/foundations. Membership does not confer ownership shares or personal rights to the association's wealth. [S22, PDF pp. 5, 12]

### Local Club drafting framework in the August 2023 model

**Status: historical model/template, not evidence of a particular Club's adopted rules.** This document proposes a full local constitution covering name/address, objectives/restrictions, membership, admissions/transfers, discipline, governing board, elections, committees, officer roles, assets, income, expenditure, budgets, audit, assemblies, honours, dissolution and amendments. Its signature/certification page is designed to record local adoption and international approval. It contains term-length blanks, older international governance and substantive provisions superseded by the 2025 Statutes/April 2026 By-laws. Local law, the current international hierarchy and the Club's approved adopted text must be checked before using its numbers operationally. [S34, PDF pp. 2–4, 10–17]

**Officers and delivery structure:** the model proposes President/Chair, one/two Vice Presidents, Secretary, Treasurer and Development/Communications or outgoing-President role, with optional PR, publicity/sponsorship, programmes/events and Fund functions especially in Clubs of at least 25 members. Those functions and committee members form a wider Governing Board. A paid administrator can assist and speak as an observer without voting. President executes Assembly/Board decisions, represents the Club, welcomes visiting members and reports annually; legally binding Club documents bear the President's signature jointly with another Board member. Vice Presidents assist/deputise. Secretary drafts minutes/notices/correspondence, prepares agendas/reports, jointly approves applications with President and maintains statutes and database records. Treasurer collects/remits dues, manages accounts/payments and submits annual plus monthly/quarterly reports. [S34, PDF pp. 10–13]

**Model election and Board mechanics:** elections occur at the AGM or an extraordinary meeting; normal service begins the next 1 January, with extraordinary-election exceptions. The template leaves one/two-year term choices, proposes President/VP one further term, Secretary up to six years and other non-representative officers up to four. It proposes two-year prior membership for Board/Auditor candidacy, individual rather than bloc candidatures, secret ballots, 50%+1 in the first ballot and most votes in a second, with nominations thirty days before the meeting and floor nominations if none were received. Proposed Board meetings are at least six annually, callable also by one-quarter of Board officers; quorum is 50% of the full Board; majority decides and President breaks a tie. Three consecutive unjustified Board absences imply resignation, with interim vacancy filling to next Assembly. These are **model local provisions**, not the international Board's quorum/ballot rules, and its Retired-office restrictions and President-office-combination prohibition conflict with newer international texts. [S34, PDF pp. 10–12; S24, PDF pp. 12–13]

**Model accounts and audit:** income may include dues, fundraising, sponsorship, donations and other lawful receipts. Local AGM fixes category dues taking international/NC obligations into account. Funds serve the Club's non-profit purposes; members have no personal rights in assets. Annual budget includes delegate travel and goes to Assembly; proposed fiscal year is calendar year unless local law prevents it. Expenditure normally requires Board approval, with a justified President/Treasurer emergency approval subsequently submitted to Board. The model calls for at least one, preferably two, independent local Auditors, outside the Board, checking records and reporting monthly/quarterly plus annually; either can report irregularities or request extraordinary meeting. Article 20 separately proposes electing two Auditors with staggered two-year terms, so the model itself is not perfectly uniform on minimum auditor numbers. [S34, PDF pp. 10, 12–14]

**Model assemblies:** AGM is proposed in the year's last quarter with thirty-day notice and fifteen-day circulation of accounts, budget, audit and candidates. Model first-call quorum is **more than 50%** of qualifying members; a second call thirty minutes later requires **30%**; if still absent, a meeting within the next two weeks uses members actually present. It proposes no proxy/mail votes, secret ballot for individuals or on request of at least 25% of present voting members, signed minutes and attendance lists. Extraordinary meetings may follow two-thirds Board request, an Auditor's specified request or one-third Active-member request, scheduled within four weeks with fifteen-day detailed notice. Virtual/hybrid participation needs certifiable attendance/votes. These model thresholds do not establish an individual Club's operative quorum and must not be confused with international AGA quorum. [S34, PDF pp. 14–15]

**Model adoption/amendment/dissolution:** additional internal regulations must not contradict higher rules. Local amendment proposals normally reach Secretary forty-five days before the meeting and require two-thirds of valid votes; obligatory consequential amendments from international changes follow a separate notice route. Local statutes/major changes need international approval, and a national-law conflict must be documented and referred to Secretariat/Governance. For voluntary termination the model requires a purpose-called extraordinary meeting with two-thirds of eligible members present and two-thirds of those present approving; debts are paid before remaining assets move under NC/international jurisdiction, with a model three-year asset-return provision on reactivation. These are historical template mechanisms, subject to current superior rules, applicable law and actual local adoption. [S34, PDF pp. 16–17]

## Conduct, conflicts, sanctions and appeals

### Historical human-rights position and advocacy

The undated **Human Rights** article quotes then-World President Mok Singh and urges the travel industry to treat human rights with the seriousness already given to environmental sustainability. It advocates asking suppliers about their own and destination human-rights practices during contracting, making travellers aware of those issues, supporting destinations that respect residents' rights and involve indigenous communities in tourism decisions, and turning organisational affiliations/logos into practical action. It identifies protection of women/men/children from abuse, poverty alleviation and protection of children in tourism as relevant concerns. [S31, PDF pp. 1–2]

The article **proposes**, rather than demonstrates implementation of, an industry human-rights code and a publicly discussable list of non-compliant countries, regions and companies; it invites industry, NGOs and other interested parties to contribute. It does not supply an enacted code, verified blacklist, binding procurement test or current country assessment. Its **18,000-member** figure, then-president identity, UNWTO-era terminology and contacts are historical context, not verified current organisational data. An AI can explain the stated ethical direction but must not invent a present ban, endorsement, rights ranking or adopted enforcement programme. [S31, PDF pp. 1–2]

### Professional conduct and declared interests

The supplied Code applies to Directors, Deputies and Auditors, including candidates/signatories. Values are integrity, honesty, prudence, loyalty, respect and equity. It prohibits advancing private interests through office, abusive conduct and damaging disclosures; requires participation, sharing relevant organisational information, prompt reporting of violations/illegal acts, adherence to Spanish law and Skål rules, and confidentiality **after office ends**. Its scope is specific; do not casually describe every member as a signatory to this director/auditor contract. [S27, PDF pp. 1–3]

A conflict may be **real, apparent or potential** and include personal gain/loss, relatives, partners or an organisation in which the person has a paid/voluntary role. It must be disclosed promptly **in writing**, minuted, and followed by abstention from relevant discussion and decisions. The declaration records the entity, relationship, potential conflict and signature, or declares no conflicts; use a separate declaration per case and update when circumstances change. [S27, PDF pp. 1–3; S28, PDF pp. 1–2]

Code breaches can be examined by five Governance Committee members at President/Vice President request, or an exceptional mediation team. The subject receives written notice and a chance to be heard and submit material; recommendations go to the Board, which decides. The Code's phrase **“assumption of office for a fixed period”** in its sanction clause is ambiguous; do not silently reinterpret it as an exact suspension power without official clarification. Apply higher-authority statutory hearing/appeal requirements. [S27, PDF p. 3; S22, PDF p. 6]

### Member disciplinary procedure

**Club level:** written notice, hearing, absolute majority of committee members and written reasoned decision within **15 days after hearing**. Categories: reprimand, suspension and expulsion. After a warning, ordinary suspension can last up to **six months**; seriously prejudicial conduct can justify **six to twelve months** without a prior reprimand. Appeal is to next Club General Assembly; if it will not meet within two months, international intervention can be sought. Source wording delays effect pending final decision. Club expulsion is for highly prejudicial conduct or continuation after a suspension longer than six months; appeal to international Board within **one month**, with expulsion stayed pending final decision if appealed. Expelled members receive no dues refund and cannot join another Club. [S24, PDF p. 25]

**National level:** NC can issue a reprimand to a Club with prior international Board knowledge; suspension of Clubs remains exclusively international Board authority. [S24, PDF p. 25]

**International level:** hearing, reasoned official notice and the specified Board vote are required. Collective sanctions can reprimand a Skål body or dismiss its officers, with temporary Secretariat supervision/replacement committee. Personal sanctions include reprimand, suspension up to **one year** and expulsion. Appeals against international sanctions are submitted **within 15 days of receipt**, in writing through Secretariat, to an Appeals Board comprising the **three most recent international Past Presidents willing to serve who are outside the originating Region**. They work virtually without cost and rule within **one month**. The By-laws describe the Appeals Board decision on expulsion as final, while Statutes generally preserve appeal of Board decisions to next Assembly; obtain authority advice for a disputed route. [S24, PDF pp. 25–26; S22, PDF p. 8]

## Welfare, honours and branding

### Florimond Volckaert Fund (FVF)

Named for the movement's first President, FVF provides **grants for personal distress**, not membership-growth projects. Under April 2026 rules eligible current-card holders are Active, Active Individual, Allied, Life, Retired and Young Skål Professional members and their immediate family (**spouse and biological/adopted children**). Families of members who held a valid card at death may also qualify without a time limit. Grounds include death, serious/prolonged illness, otherwise unaffordable medical expenses, accident, criminal damage, disability equipment and natural disasters or similarly justified needs not covered by insurance/other means. The rules exclude property damage and cases with a specific applicable aid fund. Do not promise eligibility, an amount or automatic payment. Student eligibility is not explicitly included in this detailed 2026 list although Statutes refer more broadly to Young Skål. [S24, PDF pp. 30–31; S22, PDF p. 5]

**Application:** applicant or Club President contacts the Coordinating Administrator/another Fund Administrator using the form and full circumstances, by email or website route forwarding to all three Administrators. **Do not send FVF applications to the General Secretariat.** Keep identity/correspondence confidential and involve the fewest people; wider circulation needs permission. This differs from Membership Development Fund applications, which go through the Secretariat. [S24, PDF p. 31]

Three Board-appointed Administrators manage the Fund for four-year terms, renewable once up to eight years, using majority decisions. Fund accounts are separate from ordinary association funds; payment needs Administrator request/confirmation. Donations are voluntary and promotion is a Club/NC/Area President responsibility. A single grant payment may not exceed **5% of currently available Fund total**, subject to annual review of that percentage. Report aggregate finances without revealing beneficiaries; member Auditors do not inspect personal case details, though independent audit is possible. [S24, PDF pp. 31–32]

### Membership Development Fund distinction

The **Antonio Garcia del Valle Membership Development Fund** supports Club/NC membership-growth events and initiatives, not individual distress. Administrators named in the By-laws are Vice President, membership/funds portfolio Director and Secretary. Submit requests to Secretariat **at least three weeks before the event**; approved grant payments occur **after** the event and a results report plus expense documentation. Contributions are voluntary. Consult the dedicated Fund Guide for application-level criteria; do not merge this Fund with FVF. [S24, PDF p. 14]

### Titles and awards

- **Local Honorary Member/President:** distinction remains tied to the named Club/NC/Area; normally at least ten years of membership and distinguished service. Under By-laws, Allied honorary recognition is Club-only. It does not erase personal category or international dues. “Membre d'Honneur” is reserved for international use. [S24, PDF p. 11; S22, PDF p. 7]
- **International Honorary President:** new awards discontinued in April 2009; historical holders retain title. Past Presidents use “Past-President of Skål International” or “President Skål International [year]” while remaining members. No automatic Assembly vote; only a designated Club delegate votes. [S24, PDF p. 29]
- **Membre d'Honneur:** Active/Life/Retired with normally at least fifteen years, relevant higher-level office, leadership and exceptional contribution. Former Board members serving at least three years receive title as of right after leaving Board. Nomination needs three Clubs including the home Club, NC/Area approval, full justification/CV and submission **75 days before AGA**; one nomination per country/year, final country selection **60 days** prior if needed. Electronic election at least two weeks before AGA, then Assembly confirmation under the applicable voting procedure. Award can be posthumous; silver pin/certificate. [S24, PDF p. 29]
- **Order of Skål Merit:** President, consulting Board, may award persons/corporations for prolonged exceptional devotion/contribution. Secretariat keeps register; Board may rescind, and expulsion automatically removes honour. [S24, PDF p. 30]
- **Club of the Year:** eligible Club video due two months before World Congress opening; electronic Club vote at least two weeks before AGA, highest votes wins, announced at Congress opening. Detailed award competition rules must be checked separately. [S24, PDF p. 13]

### Trademark, name and insignia

Use the approved logotype and permitted variants in the Branding Guidelines; members may use it on business cards, email footers and social/electronic media in accordance with those rules. Only approved pins/badges may be used. The name alone “Skål International” denotes the international Assembly/Board/Secretariat; local bodies add their approved Club/NC/Area designation. Members cannot create an independently authorised corporate mark from personal membership; Corporate Affiliate and award logos are separately governed. Approved Club/NC/Area designations and permission for a Club-under-formation are controlled internationally. [S24, PDF p. 30; S22, PDF p. 4]

<a id="forming-a-new-skal-club"></a>

## Forming a new Skål club

### Purpose, membership threshold, and territorial consultation

A new club requires **at least 15 Active members**, described as executives working full-time in tourism and meeting the membership criteria. The 2026 formation guide frames the club as a local professional-tourism network connected to international friendship, collaboration, development, and sustainable/ethical tourism. Its organizational-size figures—over 12,500 members, approximately 300 clubs, 75 countries—are source-dated approximations rather than a live membership census. [S26, PDF pp. 2, 6–7]

Start by informing the National Committee if one exists. In a city/region with existing clubs, the proposed club must be at least **100 km / 62 miles** from the nearest club, measured between the official municipal centers, unless a written endorsement confirms consultation, addressed concerns, and viability without membership conflicts or poaching. The endorsement comes from the National Committee, or the concerned affiliated club where there is no National Committee. In National Committee countries, the NC must confirm in writing that relevant nearby clubs, proponents, and its board were consulted and consensus exists; send the endorsement to the Secretariat. [S26, PDF pp. 4–5]

### Recommended launch and development work

The guide recommends a tourism-representative venue and a launch presentation for potential eligible members, tourism institutions, chambers/associations, and local/travel media. Explain mission, values, benefits, and other clubs' examples. Collect expressions of interest, schedule follow-up meetings or business breakfasts, and prepare a calendar of training, technical visits, and networking. An existing mentor club can supply speakers, management/recruitment advice, and practical pre-foundation membership experience. Partnerships with tourism institutions and business associations can strengthen credibility, resources, and contacts. These are recommended development methods, not substitutes for the approval requirements. [S26, PDF pp. 3–4]

### Formation submission checklist

Send the following to **membership@skal.org**, using current Secretariat forms where available:

1. **Official notification:** lead organizer's full name, correspondence email, proposed club denomination, country, city/region membership scope, and confirmation of National Committee notification where applicable. [S26, PDF p. 5]
2. **Territorial endorsement:** the written consultation/consensus evidence described above when applicable. [S26, PDF p. 5]
3. **Legal registration:** register under local law; open a club-name bank account with authorized signatories, usually President and Treasurer; submit the official registration certificate. [S26, PDF p. 5]
4. **Official name:** “Skål International [City/Region]”. Local registration law may impose a different legal format, but Skål database, branding, and invoicing use the prescribed organizational denomination. Tell the Secretariat the proposed name so it can appear in the application form. During formation, use “Skål International [Name] (Under Formation)” for promotion until Executive Board approval. [S26, PDF p. 6]
5. **Club statutes:** adopt the official Model Statutes supplied by the Secretariat, complete Articles 1 and 20, and have the proposed President and Secretary sign the final page. Submit any local-law-required modifications for Secretariat review and approval. [S26, PDF p. 6]
6. **Board:** provide at least a President, one or two Vice Presidents, Secretary, and Treasurer; submit the board appointment/election minutes and a copy of the Club President's identity document through the appropriate administrative channel. [S26, PDF p. 6]
7. **Founders:** list at least 15 eligible founding executives, each completing the official membership application under **Join Skål**. The Secretariat evaluates eligibility and approves formation-stage applications; once operating, the Club President and Secretary also approve subsequent applications through the platform. [S26, PDF pp. 6–7]
8. **Payments:** remit the one-time **€300 club entrance fee**, plus per-member international membership and administration fees according to the relevant year and joining period. Budget separately for local costs and any National/Area Committee dues. [S26, PDF pp. 7–8]
9. **Club corporate email:** create a club-owned organizational address rather than a personal or employer-dependent address. This becomes the official global-database contact and receives invoices and certificates, preserving continuity through leadership changes. Secretariat mass mailings still go to member email addresses. [S26, PDF p. 8]
10. **Additional compliance:** all members must understand the Statutes/By-laws and accept the Code of Ethics upon application; boards must know and disseminate it. Clubs in jurisdictions classified as non-cooperative by Spanish tax authorities must complete specific anti-money-laundering documentation. [S26, PDF p. 4]

### Approval, chartering, and inaugural-event planning

After receipt of required documents and payments, the Secretariat requests **Executive Board approval**. Approved clubs and members enter the global database; certificates and invoices are shared with the club; members receive welcome/login information and a smartphone-downloadable virtual card. The club receives its charter, complimentary founder pins, and a presidential chain. Supply the full shipping street address, telephone, and recipient ID requested by the Secretariat. Allow **at least two weeks** in planning the inaugural event for processing and shipping; this is a planning allowance, not a delivery guarantee. [S26, PDF p. 8]

### Active-membership industry boundaries in the formation guide

The formation guide requires full-time managerial tourism roles such as President/Chair/Owner/CEO; Vice President/Managing Director/Director General/General Manager; Assistant/Deputy equivalents; Director/Deputy Director; or Manager/Deputy Manager, including relevant functional departments. The current By-laws remain the controlling eligibility source; job title alone does not remove activity requirements or exclusions. [S26, PDF p. 7]

Its detailed activity taxonomy covers:

- **Accommodation:** hotel groups/brands and accommodation providers; excludes tourist apartments and holiday guest houses. [S26, PDF p. 9]
- **Attractions and experiences:** visitor-oriented golf/resorts, gastronomy/beverage tourism, leisure/heritage/entertainment attractions, medical tourism, nature/adventure, tourism-focused restaurants, sport tourism, wellness/spa/retreats. Exclusions include primarily resident/member golf clubs, tasting that is merely retail sales, catering, individual guides/instructors, local-only activities, quick-takeaway or beverage/nightlife-led operations, and chefs/maîtres who are not proprietors. Medical-tourism facilitators have specified association-registration requirements. [S26, PDF pp. 9–10]
- **Operational and strategic services:** congress/exhibition venues, professional congress/event organizers, tourism-exclusive consulting/professionals, travel-only insurance, tourism-focused marketing/communications, and travel technology/booking platforms. General cross-industry providers do not become Active-eligible merely by having some tourism customers; supplier/Allied criteria must be assessed separately. [S26, PDF pp. 10–11]
- **Travel services:** tourism-impacting arts/cultural event organizers; DMCs; inbound/outbound tour operators; agencies and OTAs. Travel brokers must be contracted with an IATA-registered organization such as a wholesaler, consolidator, or tour operator. Fixed museums/heritage sites belong under attractions, rather than the event-organizer classification. [S26, PDF p. 12]
- **Transportation:** passenger airlines/airports/GSAs, licensed tourism car/mobility rental, tourism coach/bus, cruise/water transport/ports, and tourism/long-distance rail. Excludes freight-only transport, taxis/ride-hailing, courier/guide individuals, and primarily commuter/local public transport. [S26, PDF pp. 12–13]
- **Tourist/travel organizations:** hospitality associations, destination/tourism governance bodies, travel-trade/MICE associations, and tourism-linked passenger-transport associations. [S26, PDF p. 13]
- **Media and education:** tourism-specific certification, accredited tourism/hospitality institutions, proven tourism leadership speakers/executive coaches, and travel editorial media. Eligible academic roles are heads/deans/principals/directors and permanent senior lecturers; visiting/part-time/general staff are excluded. Eligible media roles are full-time publisher, editor-in-chief, journalist, producer, or broadcaster dedicated to tourism/travel; personal/non-editorial influencers/bloggers and freelance/part-time journalists are excluded. [S26, PDF p. 14]

Across the plans, recurring themes are friendship as a basis for trusted professional relationships; visible membership value; recruitment coupled with retention; professional development; international rather than exclusively local engagement; community service; sound finances; more effective governance; accurate membership data; and digital tools that improve communication and administration. ### Digital transformation: 2017 background

The October 2017 roadmap was commissioned from Bip. Its method was **AS IS** audit/diagnosis, **TO BE** target model, and prioritized **ROADMAP**. The diagnosis called for stronger in-house IT/database expertise, clearer staff responsibilities, safer email and IT security, a mobile-responsive website, document/project/helpdesk tools, data reporting, CRM, supplier service-level agreements, and club participation in digital workflows. These are dated diagnostic findings rather than claims about present-day security or architecture. [S17, PDF pp. 6–9]

The target model proposed CEO-led digital strategy supported by trained staff and external expertise; an Information Systems Manager reporting to the CEO; redesigned processes before digitization; and tools for CRM, the Skål Business Application (SBA), business intelligence, project management, helpdesk, file storage, and website interactions. It explicitly identified lead generation, member approval, invoicing/cancellations, retention, and project/activity management as processes needing definition. [S17, PDF pp. 10–12]

The roadmap's stages were launch in 2017, development in 2018, consolidation in 2019, and evolution in 2020–2022. Three short/medium-term priorities were IT capability, software tools, and website renewal. Funding approved in Hyderabad covered an initial module; further progress depended on resources and sponsorship. Do not infer a software stack, API specification, deployment architecture, or delivered implementation from this document. [S17, PDF pp. 13–16]

### Pandemic adjustment: 2021–2023

The 2021–2023 strategy responded to COVID-19 restrictions and pressure on income. It prioritized maintaining tourism-sector relevance, expanding the number and quality of global partners, and improving member services. Brand actions included institutional relationships, virtual/in-person trade shows, industry events, and a proposed virtual platform. ## World Congress: purpose, hosting, and operations

### Congress scope and how to use the 2026 manual

The annual Skål International World Congress combines the Annual General Assembly (AGA), business sessions, speakers, workshops, networking, social functions, and optional pre-/post-Congress tours. The AGA of Club delegates is the Association's supreme body; the Congress is the setting for the AGA, not a separate governing authority. Congress registration is for Skål members and their accompanying persons. Members who have not bought a Congress package may attend the AGA without charge; meals and refreshments are separately chargeable when taken. [S42, PDF pp. 4, 8, 16, 23]

The uploaded manual is titled **Congress Manual 2026**, prepared by the **2024 Congress and Meetings Committee**. It is an operational planning source, not proof that a particular destination, fee, Congress date, or supplier was actually approved. Its bid timetable still explicitly refers to voting at the **2024** Congress, and its programme contains references to proposed By-law changes. Treat these portions as dated planning text and confirm the current RFP, contract, Statutes, and By-laws before relying on a bid deadline, voting mechanism, or office-holder name. [S42, PDF pp. 1, 6, 15]

### Host destination and venue requirements

The planning window is **September to early December**, with consideration for major religious festivals, significant regional/national holidays, and major industry events. The official programme should be **no more than three nights/four days**. Destinations should review previous post-Congress survey feedback when preparing a bid. [S42, PDF p. 4]

Required or stated host capabilities include:

- Support from the host Club and, where applicable, National Committee, government/tourism authorities, and national airline; expertise for an international congress; and a qualified PCO.
- Convenient international air access, ideally no more than **one hour** from an international/major airport; inclusive entry/visa arrangements; a safe destination; nondiscrimination; and accessibility for disabled delegates.
- Ability to accommodate **up to 600 participants**, while the manual recommends budgeting a bid for **a maximum of 400 participants**. These are different planning measures, not a guaranteed attendance forecast.
- An AGA room with classroom seating for **up to 400 people**; sufficient accommodation for varied participant requirements; and preferably one main venue/hub. Alternative venues should be within a **ten-minute walk**, or, where that is not possible, within about **ten minutes by coach** of hotels.
- Complimentary Wi-Fi at official programme facilities, with access codes on badges; sponsor development that respects Skål International's existing global/yearly sponsorship agreements; and a silent auction opened before Congress, or a proposed alternative where local law prevents it.
- A sustainability plan targeting a **carbon-neutral event with plans toward net-zero emissions**, supported by monitoring processes. This is a requirement stated in the manual, not evidence that a specific Congress achieved carbon neutrality. [S42, PDF pp. 4–5, 26]
